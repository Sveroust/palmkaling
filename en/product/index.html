DOWNSTREAM SPECTRUM

What it becomes

Sugar palm fruit is more than a dessert ingredient. On the world market this arenga endosperm feeds five different industries — and each one uses a different property of the same fruit.

Photographs in this section are AI-generated illustrations of downstream product categories. They are not products we manufacture — we supply the raw material.

F&B export retail

Canned & jarred kaong

Whole kernels in light syrup, sold as kaong through Asian retail chains across the Americas and Europe. Used in fruit salads, fruit cocktails, and halo-halo.

Primary markets
United States, European Union, Australia
Property used
Uniform whole kernels that hold their shape through sterilisation

Cafe & dessert

Bingsu topping & boba alternative

Diced for Korean shaved ice and milk-based drinks. The chew holds up at cold temperatures without hardening, at a fraction of the calories of tapioca boba.

Primary markets
South Korea, Taiwan, Japan
Property used
A chewy texture that stays stable when cold

Functional food

Drinkable fibre jelly

The galactomannan in sugar palm fruit forms a hydrocolloid gel, which is why it is used as the base for low-sugar pouch jellies that keep people full for longer.

Primary markets
South Korea, Japan
Property used
Galactomannan as a gelling agent and soluble fibre

Nutraceutical

Supplement raw material

The supplement industry uses arenga galactomannan extract in capsule formulations. What they buy from us is the raw fruit, not the extract.

Primary markets
Germany, United States, South Korea
Property used
Galactomannan polysaccharide content

Cosmetics

Serum & sheet mask active

Cosmetic laboratories extract arenga polysaccharides as a water-binding ingredient for serums and sheet masks, a botanical alternative to synthetic humectants.

Primary markets
South Korea, Japan
Property used
Water-binding polysaccharides
